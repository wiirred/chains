Proof of Cost

Where FOMO falls short, what this platform can own that nobody else does, and how to build a crypto project whose utility survives after the attention leaves.

Read this caveat before you act on the FOMO section

Every primary source about FOMO is blocked from the environment this was researched in — fomo.family, its docs site, and Wikipedia all refused the connection. What follows comes from search-result summaries, and a large share of the pages that rank for “FOMO app review” are affiliate sites earning a referral commission on sign-ups. Their claims about fees and features are not independent.

Treat the FOMO column below as a hypothesis to verify, not established fact. The one finding that is robust is that sources disagree with each other — which is itself the point.

The competitor

What FOMO appears to be

A social-first, mobile crypto trading app built around discovery and copying other traders. Its bet is that the hard part of trading is knowing what to trade, so the product optimises for speed of onboarding and social signal.

Onboarding

Sign up in under a minute

Email or Apple ID, with an embedded self-custodial wallet created for you. No external wallet needed.

Social

Follow, copy, leaderboards

Trade alerts from people you follow, trades overlaid on price charts, and profitability rankings over 24h/7d/30d.

Cross-chain

One balance, “no bridges”

Trade across Solana, Base, BNB Chain and Monad from a single balance, with gasless swaps.

Pricing

A single headline fee

Reported as 0.50% per trade by some sources and a $1 flat fee by others. Gas and slippage are extra.

The opening

Where it is weak

Two kinds of gap. The structural ones come from decisions baked into the business model, and are expensive for FOMO to close. The soft ones are roadmap items they will probably ship eventually.

Structural

The headline fee is not the cost

A flat 0.50% is a clean number that describes only the named charge. Sources concede that slippage on thin pairs adds to the effective cost — which means the real cost is unstated, and on a memecoin pair the unstated part can dwarf the stated one.

Structural

Sources cannot agree what it charges

0.50% and $1 flat are wildly different products: on a $50 trade one costs 25¢ and the other costs $1. That reputable-looking pages disagree means the pricing is not legible enough to be reported consistently.

Structural

“No bridges” is an abstraction, not an absence

Value still crosses chains; the user just cannot see the mechanism or its price. Abstraction is good UX and a perfect place to hide a margin — and it removes the user's ability to decline the bridge risk.

Structural

Embedded wallet only

Great for onboarding, but hardware wallet support is reported as limited. Anyone with meaningful size keeps it on a Ledger, so the product is structurally capped at small balances.

Structural

Copying traders is a fee-generating machine

Leaderboards ranked by profit reward volatility and survivorship. The incentive is more trades, and more trades is more fee revenue — so the product's social core and its revenue model both point away from telling people when not to trade.

Roadmap

Card funding still “coming soon”

Debit and credit funding is unshipped; users deposit crypto or use Apple Pay in some regions.

Roadmap

Portfolio analytics described as basic

Reviewers repeatedly call the analytics thin. No cost-basis reporting, no realised-versus-unrealised breakdown.

Roadmap

No advanced order types

Market orders in a social feed. No limit orders, stops, or scheduled execution.

Roadmap

Support and reliability complaints

Reports of trouble selling and withdrawing, generic support replies, and lag at peak. Ordinary scaling pain.

The wedge

Why they cannot simply copy you

A venue that itemises the spread cannot quietly widen it.

Most trading revenue in crypto is not the disclosed fee — it is the difference between the price you got and the price that existed. Publishing that number as a line item, next to the disclosed fee, converts a soft margin into a public commitment.

That is why transparency is not a feature a competitor bolts on in a sprint. It is a constraint they would have to accept on their own revenue. Anyone can add a social feed to your product in a month; nobody can add your ledger to theirs without giving something up.

The build

Four things nobody currently offers

Days

The cost odometer

A running total of what trading has actually cost this wallet — this month, this year, since you started — broken down by gas, spread, bridge and platform fee. Every venue shows you your profit. None will tell you what the venue itself took.

Days

The same trade, priced elsewhere

Beside every quote: what this trade would cost on a flat-0.50% venue, on a 1% venue, and at cost. Pure arithmetic on numbers already in hand, and it makes the comparison concrete instead of rhetorical.

Days

Receipts that survive the session

Signed, exportable records with tx hashes, itemised costs and cost basis — CSV for a spreadsheet, JSON for an accountant. Crypto tax software reconstructs this badly from chain data. You have it first-hand.

Weeks

Honest execution scoring

Because you record estimated versus actual on every trade, you can grade routers and bridges on how often their quotes hold. Nobody can publish that credibly about themselves — but a venue that measures everyone, including itself, can.

The crypto project

Utility that outlasts the attention

You asked for a real product with real utility that survives without hype. The honest starting point: most tokens fail because the product would work identically without them. So the test for anything here is whether it does something the product genuinely needs.

The asset you are accumulating is not a token. It is a dataset nobody else can produce: verified, first-hand records of what trades actually cost, across venues and chains, measured rather than advertised. That compounds with every trade, it is useful to people who will never care about crypto ideology, and it gets more valuable as it gets older.

Receipts, local and private

No token · no backend · shipping now

Every trade produces an itemised, exportable record. Value to the user is immediate and needs nobody else to participate. This is the part that must be good before anything else is built.

The public cost index

No token · opt-in publishing

Users can publish a receipt, signed by the wallet that made the trade, stripped of identity. Aggregate them into a public index of real execution cost by pair, chain, router and venue. Anyone can verify a receipt against the chain, which is what separates this from self-reported marketing numbers.

Cost-aware routing

No token · the index feeding back

Once you know which routers' quotes hold and which venues quietly widen, route around the bad ones. The product gets measurably cheaper because of the dataset — the flywheel that makes the whole thing defensible.

A token, only if the index needs one

Deliberately deferred

The one job a token could genuinely do here is pay for adversarial verification: stake to attest that a batch of receipts is accurate, and let anyone challenge a false one and take the stake. That is a real coordination problem — but only once the index is big enough that lying about it is worth someone's money. Launching it before then means the token is the product, which is the failure mode you asked to avoid.

Ship phases one through three with no token at all.

If the index becomes something people rely on, the token has an obvious job and an obvious value. If it does not, you have still built a genuinely useful product and you have not sold anyone a claim on nothing. Every phase is worth doing on its own terms — that is the actual test of durable utility.

Maki

How this connects to the lore, and how it shouldn't

A caution worth stating plainly: attaching a financial product's value to a fictional universe is the exact mechanism by which hype-driven projects fail. If the token's worth depends on people caring about the story, then when they stop caring, it goes to zero — regardless of how good the trading product is.

The version that works keeps them separate in substance and joined in surface. Maki is the brand, the voice, the reason someone remembers the name and enjoys being there. The cost index is the thing that has to stand on its own spreadsheet. A good analogy is any company with a beloved mascot: the mascot brings people in, and the product is why they stay. Nobody values the company on the mascot.

Concretely, that means the lore can own the identity, the community, the interface personality and the cosmetics — and should stay out of the token's value accrual, the fee schedule, and anything a user has to understand to know what a trade costs them.
